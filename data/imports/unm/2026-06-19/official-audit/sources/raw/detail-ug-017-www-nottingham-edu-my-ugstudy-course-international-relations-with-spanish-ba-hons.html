Course overview

The BA (Hons) International Relations with Spanish is a unique programme combining in-depth study of global politics with advanced Spanish language proficiency. Designed for students seeking careers in diplomacy, international organisations, or global business, this three-year full-time course equips students with the analytical, research, and communication skills essential for navigating the complexities of the modern international landscape.

Students will explore core themes in international relations, including diplomacy, global security, political economy, and regional politics, while mastering Spanish through progressive modules that integrate language and cultural studies. The curriculum features research-led teaching, diverse assessments, and opportunities for independent projects, fostering critical thinking and practical problem-solving skills.

Why choose this course?

  • Comprehensive Learning: Gain an in-depth understanding of global politics, diplomacy, international security, and global economic systems.
  • Language Mastery in Spanish: Achieve fluency in Spanish, enhancing employability in international organisations, diplomacy, and global business.
  • Research Opportunities: Engage in research-led modules and independent projects exploring diverse international relations topics.
  • Global Exposure: Study at Nottingham Malaysia with the option for exchange programmes at our UK or Ningbo campuses.
  • Cultural Proficiency: Deepen cultural understanding through Spanish studies, enriching your global perspective.
  • Innovative Curriculum: Experience a blend of theoretical grounding and real-world application through modern teaching tools and methodologies.

Core Modules

This module aims to provide students with the basics for the study of politics and international relations, doing so mostly from a historical perspective. Indeed, any analysis of modern-day world affairs is not possible without students having a strong grasp of contemporary global history. The module therefore aims to provide students with a framework of some of the main developments in world affairs since 1945, a knowledge of the background factors which influenced these developments, and an understanding of the historiographical debates surrounding them, and through these to develop the intellectual and transferable skills.

By the end of the module you will:

  • Be able to appreciate the nature and complexity of global politics.
  • Have a grasp of the theoretical underpinnings of the major approaches to global politics.
  • Have a good working knowledge of the strengths and weaknesses of the major approaches to global politics.
  • Be familiar with the core concepts and controversies in analysing global politics.

  • Knowledge and understanding: knowledge of foundations of the definition and explanations of global politics.

    This module has two components to it: one relating to the art of doing business in Southeast Asia in terms of its traditions, systems and society, while the other will serve to examine regional political economy strategies.

    The first half of the module will strive to identify the micro and macro forces shaping international business between Southeast Asia and other countries, both in supporting and resisting globalisation. Simultaneously, it will explain how businesses operate in Southeast Asia from a “stakeholder” perspective of market, non-market, internal and external perspectives.

    The second half will assist students in analysing the patterns and characteristics of political, economic and cultural change in Southeast Asia. It will investigate common and competing understandings of economy-state-society relations in the region. After briefly reviewing the region's modern history from a political economy vantage point, the part of the module will look at a number of topical issues in the region such as the politics of financial development, markets/consumption cultures, people/mobilities and the emergence of new forms of regional cooperation.

    This Environmental Politics module provides a comprehensive exploration of the key concepts, themes, and debates within the field. It begins with an introduction to the foundational aspects of environmental politics, including the historical roots and development of environmental thought. Students will delve into various political ideologies such as ecologism, and examine the ethical dimensions of environmental issues, including environmental justice and the moral status of non-human entities. The course also covers the relationship between democracy, the state, and environmental governance, highlighting the role of public participation and policy-making processes. Additionally, students will explore different methods of valuing the environment, both economically and non-economically, and critically analyse the concept of sustainable development.

    In the latter part of the course, students will address the phenomenon of climate change denial and its impact on environmental politics, as well as the role of social movements in driving environmental transformation. The course emphasizes the importance of hope and inspiration for a sustainable future, encouraging students to envision and work towards a more equitable world. Through a combination of lectures, case study analyses, debates, workshops, and creative projects, students will engage deeply with the material and develop a nuanced understanding of the complexities and challenges in environmental politics. The course concludes with a review of key concepts and final reflections, allowing students to consolidate their knowledge and share their insights.

    The Silk Road is an excellent example of a series of linked communities across a wide geographical space. The connections between them changed at different times. This discipline-bridging module will develop an appreciation of how the different disciplines that focus on the Silk Road overlap and inter-relate with each other. Thus, scientific and non-scientific disciplines will be combined. An example is the combination of the contributions from history, archaeology, geography and archaeological science. Such a combination will illuminate and inform the ways in which the movement of peoples and things along the Silk Road over time occurred and helped to define it. Interpretation of the evidence is frequently not particularly straightforward. This module is designed to make students ask questions about the evidence that they are presented with. By the end of the module knowledge of discipline-bridging methodologies will be gained on a temporal and geographical scale that students rarely encounter in other modules.

    This module has two complementary objectives to it: to allow students to better understand contemporary human rights issues, on top of learning about the role, function and impact of civil society groups in the context of global human rights advocacy.

    On the former, this module further develops the connections between global and local causes and responses to contemporary human rights issues. The overall focus of the module is on exploring evolving political and legal strategies to advance human rights in a global political framework. The module examines the relevance of debates about the possible emergence of a global civil society to understanding human rights practices, continuing the themes of regulation to examine the expansion and role of transnational human rights monitoring and activism. The module recognises the need for an understanding of the debate about the relationship between democratisation, development, human rights and violence in different political contexts around the world, both in long-established democracies and in the majority world/global South.

    On the latter, the module will also explore civil society. Historically-speaking, civil society groups have not simply exerted pressure upon the state but on many occasions even defined its very contours. Under conditions of constitutional crises, civil society movements have become decisive and transformative. Anti-colonial struggles are an obvious example. In other areas, ranging from race and the environment, to women’s rights, labour rights, human rights, civil liberties, etc., civil society groups have shaped legislative outcomes that are now taken for granted. Their legal strategies, tactics, successes – and failures – are a significant theme in this course. The aim of this module is to place these forces into theoretical, historical, contemporary and comparative perspective. Using case studies, the module examines civil society and activism in relation to the promotion and defence of human rights. The School strongly expects active participation from local civil society groups in the design and delivery of this module.

    • CBSE/CISCE Class XII English with the required grades will only be accepted if applicants provide a confirmation letter stating that English was the medium of instruction in Class XII.
    • English language tests must be taken at a test centre situated in the applicant’s country of residence.
    • International applicants who meet the University’s requirements may also need to submit extra documents or an approved English test for MOHE, if required.
